## Read this before using the chapter

Chapter 16 has more content that cannot be served than any chapter extracted
so far — more than marriage's ch.18. Four separate exclusions, each with its
own reason, plus one class that needed the same age-literalism correction
marriage's timing block did.

### 1. Two shlokas make paternity/legitimacy claims — excluded entirely

**S 16.14** ("the child so born is of questionable birth") and **S 16.15**
("the native is undoubtedly born of other's loins") state that a chart
combination indicates a child is not the claimed father's. There is no neutral
astrological core to preserve here — the claim itself, that a specific
combination proves illegitimacy, is the objectionable content, not a framing of
it. Same category as marriage's obedience rules (ch.24.76, 82): excluded, not
rephrased. Santhanam's own notes acknowledge the combination "gets nullified
under certain conditions" and defers to a different text for details — even the
translator treats it as unsettled.

### 2. The sex-of-children rule is excluded on real-world-harm grounds

**S 16.13** reads a 5th lord conjunct or in the Moon's decanate as indicating
more daughters than sons. This is not treated the same as a gendered-language
problem (which the "spouse" rewrite in the marriage KB solved by dropping the
gendered surface and keeping the rule). Here the rule's entire content **is**
a claim about the sex of unborn children, and son-preference driven by exactly
this kind of prediction has caused real, documented harm in the cultures most
of our readers come from. There is no rephrasing that keeps the astrological
claim and removes the harm; the two are the same thing. Excluded outright.
This is a stricter bar than CLAUDE.md states explicitly, applied because the
harm is specific and well documented, not hypothetical.

### 3. Child death and loss timing is `gated_death`

**S 16.21–23** give ages for losing a child (32, 33 and 36, 56). Same class as
every other longevity/mortality rule in this project: extracted so nobody
re-reads the chapter wondering if it was missed, then closed. Not retrievable;
longevity and child-mortality both refer out under CLAUDE.md regardless of the
output guard.

### 4. The exact-count-of-children block is excluded as unservable, not exempted for content reasons

**S 16.24–32** ("NUMBER OF CHILDREN") gives combinations for exactly 10, 9, 8,
7 or 1 son, several with embedded loss ("3 will pass away") and one with
remarriage framing ("offspring only through his 2nd or 3rd wife"). Unlike the
three exclusions above, this isn't primarily a harm judgement — it's a
servability one: a chart-derived exact integer count of children is the kind
of hyper-specific deterministic claim this project has avoided everywhere else
(see the marriage timing-ages problem), and several rows in the block are
`gated_death` on top of that. Excluded as a block rather than picked apart
row by row, since the death-timing and the count-claim are inseparable in most
of these shlokas.

### What is left, and it is real

Once those four are removed, a usable core remains: whether children come
easily, the general character of the parent-child relationship, adoption, and
timing framed as delay rather than a literal age. That is what is graded
below.

---

## Timing needs the same correction marriage's did

**S 16.18–20** give ages of **32/33, 30/36, and 40** for a child's birth.
Unlike marriage's split between early and delayed groups, every one of these
ages is late relative to typical fertility timing — there is no "early" bucket
in this chapter. Read as **the chart indicating a later path to children, not a
literal age**, for the same reason marriage's ages could not be read literally:
a specific year is a 17th-century social-calendar artifact, and treating it as
a modern prediction (especially one this specific and this late) risks reading
as a fertility-anxiety trigger rather than a description of tendency.

## The rules worth serving

### Whether children come
- **S 16.12, p.109** — a strong 5th lord aspected by strong Mercury, Jupiter and
  Venus indicates many children. `[safe]`
- **S 16.16, p.109** — a 5th lord that is exalted, in the 2nd/5th/9th from lagna,
  or conjunct/aspected by Jupiter indicates children will come. Santhanam's
  note: any one of the three conditions is sufficient on its own. `[safe]`
- **S 16.17, p.110** — three or four malefics in the 5th with a debilitated 5th
  lord indicates behavioural challenges in the children; a benefic (including
  Mercury) in the 5th cancels the combination. Framed as a flag on the
  children's tendencies, same treatment CLAUDE.md gives a dosha — never a
  verdict. `[safe]`

### Adoption
- **S 16.11, p.109** — the 5th house tenanted by six planets, its lord in the
  12th, with a strong Moon and lagna, indicates adopted issue. This maps
  directly onto the taxonomy's `adoption` subdomain. `[safe]`

### Timing, framed as delay not a date
- **S 16.18–20, p.110** — see "Timing needs the same correction" above.
  `[safe]`, framed as delayed timing only.

**Same shape as every prior chapter.** Roughly half of the twelve placements
are genuinely about children; the rest are wealth, siblings, standing and
temperament. The 5th lord is a significator of intelligence, merit and
purva-punya (past-life credit) as much as of progeny — stated here so an agent
does not force every placement into a parenting frame.
