## Read this before using the chapters

Chapter 13 is short (13 shlokas) and unusually clean — no gendered-authorship
problem, no prurient material, no death content of its own. Chapter 22 (11
shlokas) is short too, but carries a pattern this project has handled before
in a different domain: **literal ages and literal currency amounts**, the
same class of over-precision marriage's and children's timing verses needed
softening for.

### 1. Chapter 22's literal ages and Nishka amounts cannot be read literally

Four of chapter 22's eleven shlokas name a specific age (33rd, 36th, 40th
year) and a specific quantity of **Nishkas** — an archaic gold-coin unit,
meaningless to a modern reader and not convertible to anything the reader
could sanity-check. Serving "you will gain 2000 Nishkas in your 36th year" as
if it were a real prediction is worse than the marriage timing-ages problem:
at least an age is something a reader can relate to their own life; a
currency unit that stopped being used centuries ago has no honest modern
reading at all.

**These four rows keep the chart combination and drop both the age and the
amount**, reading as "a notable gain indicated" rather than serving false
precision on either axis. Each ships `convention_dependent` with an
`observance_note` saying so explicitly — this is a softening decision, not a
disputed-source one, and the note says which.

### 2. Chapter 24's excluded rows, and why each one specifically

- **S 24.15 (2nd lord in the 3rd)** — excluded. The verse's content is
  character judgment ("lustful and miserly") plus "will earn through
  prostitutes," which has no wealth-specific core worth extracting once the
  character material is set aside.
- **S 24.19 (2nd lord in the 7th)** — excluded. The verse's actual content is
  a claim about the spouse's (or, per a second reading the Notes mention, the
  mother's) fidelity — "of questionable character." Same category as
  marriage's excluded control/obedience rules: the claim itself is the
  problem, there is no neutral wealth rule sitting underneath it.
- **S 24.22 (2nd lord in the 10th)** — excluded as off-topic, not gated. The
  verse is almost entirely about temperament and filial happiness
  ("libidinous, honourable, learned... bereft of filial happiness"); "much
  wealth" is one clause in a sentence that is otherwise a character
  chapter's material sitting inside the wealth lord's list. Same shape the
  career and health extractions already documented — a house or lord is a
  significator cluster, not a single-topic label, and roughly half of any
  chapter's placements land somewhere else.
- **S 24.125 (11th lord in the 5th)** — excluded as off-topic. Entirely about
  children's happiness and education; belongs with the children domain, not
  wealth.
- **S 24.126 (11th lord in the 6th)** — excluded as off-topic. Primarily
  disease, temperament and living-abroad content; the Notes add a passing
  "will often undergo financial reversals," but that fragment is inseparable
  from the surrounding health/character material it's embedded in, not a
  clean standalone wealth rule.
- **S 24.128 (11th lord in the 8th)** — `gated_death`. The verse's point is
  the native's own longevity and that "his wife will predecease him" — a
  spouse-mortality claim built into the same sentence as the longevity
  claim. Same treatment every other domain's death-adjacent material gets:
  extracted here so nobody re-reads the chapter wondering if it was missed,
  then closed. Not retrievable.
- **S 24.130 (11th lord in the 10th)** — excluded as off-topic. Almost
  entirely religious reputation and character ("honoured by the king...
  attached to his religion... truthful and will subdue his senses"); no
  wealth-specific claim in it at all.

### 3. Gendered plural-spouse and pejorative-translation phrasing, reworded not dropped

**S 24.14** ("will have two or more wives") and **S 24.132** ("will have many
wives and will befriend barbarians") use the same 17th-century assumption of
a male native marriage's chapter 18 already documented — the underlying
chart claim (more than one significant partnership; foreign connections) is
kept, the gendered surface is dropped, following the identical "spouse" /
"partner" rewrite the marriage KB established. **S 24.132**'s "barbarians" is
additionally just Santhanam's own dated word choice for "foreigners" and is
dropped as translation baggage, not source content — the underlying shloka
says nothing that needs a pejorative English word to carry it.

## The rules worth serving

### Chapter 13 — condition of the 2nd lord and 2nd house
- **S 13.1–2, p.96** — the 2nd lord in the 2nd, an angle, or a trine
  promotes wealth; in the 6th, 8th or 12th it declines. A benefic in the 2nd
  supports wealth, a malefic there works against it. `[safe]`
- **S 13.3, p.96** — Jupiter as the 2nd lord placed in the 2nd, or Jupiter
  conjunct Mars, indicates wealth. `[safe]`
- **S 13.4, p.97** — the 2nd lord in the 11th while the 11th lord is in the
  2nd (mutual exchange), or both lords together in an angle or a trine,
  indicates wealth. `[safe]`
- **S 13.5, p.97** — the 2nd lord in an angle while the 11th lord occupies a
  trine from that position, or the 2nd lord aspected by or conjunct Jupiter
  and Venus, indicates wealth. `[safe]`
- **S 13.6–7, p.97 — YOGAS FOR POVERTY** — the 2nd and 11th lords together in
  the 6th, 8th or 12th with a malefic occupying the 2nd is read as a caution
  toward financial strain, the same flag-not-verdict standard CLAUDE.md
  already gives a dosha — worth attending to, never a fixed verdict on the
  native's means. `[safe]`
- **S 13.8, p.98 — LOSS THROUGH AUTHORITY** — the 2nd and 11th lords in the
  6th/8th/12th together with Mars in the 11th and Rahu in the 2nd indicates
  financial loss through penalties or fines from an official body. Read in
  the modern sense (tax, legal or regulatory loss) per Santhanam's own note,
  which glosses "royal punishment" as exactly this. `[safe]`
- **S 13.9, p.98** — Jupiter in the 11th, Venus in the 2nd, a benefic in the
  12th, and the 2nd lord conjunct a benefic indicates expenditure toward
  religious or charitable purposes. `[safe]`
- **S 13.11, p.99 — EFFORTLESS ACQUISITION** — the 2nd lord conjunct a
  benefic and placed in a strong divisional-chart dignity indicates wealth
  acquired without significant effort, extending to the native's family.
  `[safe]`

**Same shape as every prior chapter.** Roughly half of the twelve placements
in each lord list are genuinely about wealth; the rest are family, health,
character or another domain entirely. The 2nd lord is as much a significator
of speech, family and the right eye as of money; the 11th lord is as much a
significator of elder siblings and social gain as of wealth specifically.
Read the placement, do not assume the topic — the same lesson every domain
extraction in this project has landed on independently.

## What this pass does not cover

Wealth's taxonomy lists seven subdomains: `income`, `savings`, `speculation`,
`inheritance`, `debts`, `losses`, `poverty_combinations`. Chapters 13 and 22
are squarely about **income** and, in their negative-combination shlokas,
**poverty_combinations** and **losses** — nothing here speaks to savings,
speculation, inheritance or debts specifically. Those four subdomains remain
covered only by the domain's three pre-existing generic references. The
natural next extraction pass for this domain is the Dhana Yoga chapter(s)
and, per `docs/kb_corpus_sources.md`, Saravali (94.9% OCR accuracy, the best
in the corpus and still entirely untouched) as this domain's first
cross-check source once a second pass exists to check against.
