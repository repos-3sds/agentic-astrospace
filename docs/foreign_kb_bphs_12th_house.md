## Why this extract is short, stated plainly rather than padded

Career, health, marriage and children each get a full BPHS chapter built
around their house. **Foreign residence does not.** Chapter 23 (the 12th
house) is built almost entirely around *expenditure* — the 12th's primary
classical signification — with only two shlokas that speak to foreign
residence directly. The 12th-lord placement series in ch.24 (shlokas 134–144)
was checked in full and is likewise about expenditure in each of the twelve
houses, not travel.

The 3rd house (short journeys in modern usage) and 9th house (long journeys,
foreign settlement, in modern usage) were both checked chapter by chapter for
this translation and **contain no explicit statement about foreign travel or
residence** — those associations are things later, secondary literature
layers onto the houses, not something this primary text states. Rather than
attribute a foreign-travel rule to the 9th or 3rd house on the strength of
common practice this text does not itself support, nothing from those
chapters is included here.

**This domain needs a different source for real depth.** The standing
candidates are Saravali (94.9%, untouched) and Prasna Marga — already cited
once in the existing seed references for this domain — neither yet checked
against this question specifically.

## The two rules the text actually gives

- **S 23.11, p.144** — the 12th lord and 12th house both afflicted by, and
  aspected by, malefics indicates movement from country to country. `[safe]`
- **S 23.12, p.144** — the 12th lord and 12th house both associated with, and
  aspected by, benefics indicates the native stays within their own country.
  `[safe]`

Santhanam's own note on these two shlokas: they are given as a pair and are
meant to be read against each other — the same combination inverted between
malefic and benefic association is what separates settling abroad from
staying home, not two independent rules.
