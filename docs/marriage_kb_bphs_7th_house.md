## Read this before using the chapter

Chapter 18 is the most content-sensitive material extracted so far, and it
cannot be lifted verbatim. Three separate problems, handled three different
ways.

### 1. It is written for a male native with a wife

Every rule assumes it. "The wife will be sickly", "plurality of wives", "union
with many women", "he will beget a disobedient wife". Our readers are not all
men, and the gender is **the 17th-century reader's assumption, not a fact the
chart states** — the underlying rule is about the condition of the 7th house and
its lord. So the structure is kept and the gendered surface is dropped: *the
spouse*, *the partner*, *the marriage*.

This is not softening the astrology. A rule that reads "the 7th lord in the 6th,
8th or 12th indicates a sickly wife" says something specific about the 7th
lord in dusthanas; "wife" adds nothing to that and excludes half our readers.

### 2. Some rules have no neutral core and are excluded outright

**Control and obedience are not chart claims we can restate.** S 24.76 ("the
wife will not be under his control") and S 24.82 ("a disobedient wife") are
about a spouse's compliance with the native. There is no neutral rule inside
them to keep — the claim *is* the objectionable part. Excluded, not rephrased.

**The prurient material is excluded** — S 18.9–9½ and the notes around it
describe a partner's body and specific sexual acts. No usable rule, and nothing
a reading should say.

### 3. Death of the spouse is `gated_death`

S 18.3, 18.35–39 and 18.42 give timing for a spouse's death. Same class as the
health chapters: extracted so nobody re-reads them wondering, then closed.
Longevity refers out under CLAUDE.md regardless of the output guard.

## The timing rules, and why the ages cannot be read literally

Shlokas 23–34 give marriage timing — genuinely the most useful material here,
because timing is what people ask. But the ages are:

**7, 10, 11, 12, 13, 15, 16, 18, 19** … then **22, 23, 25, 26, 27, 30, 33**

The first group are child-betrothal ages. They are what "early marriage" meant
when the text was written, not a prediction that a modern reader marries at
seven. Santhanam himself splits the block: his note at S 18.30 says the yogas
**from 30 onward "will delay one's marriage"**.

**So these read as EARLY versus DELAYED relative to the norm, never as a literal
age.** That is the rule for using them, and it is the single most important line
in this document:

| Group | Shlokas | Read as |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ages 7–19 | S 18.23–29 | marriage **earlier** than the reader's norm |
| Ages 22–33 | S 18.30–34 | marriage **later** — the text says "delay" explicitly |

An agent that says "you will marry at 11" has reproduced a 17th-century social
norm as a prediction. An agent that says "this combination points to marrying
earlier than most in your circle, and the chart has more than one such
indication" has used the same shloka honestly.

## The rules worth serving

### Condition of the seventh lord
- **S 18.2, p.121** — 7th lord in the 6th, 8th or 12th: the spouse's health is
  indicated as a concern, and the marriage lacks support. `[safe]`
- **S 18.4–5, p.122** — a 7th lord endowed with strength and well placed
  supports the marriage; weak and afflicted, it does not. `[safe]`
- **S 18.16, p.123** — the 7th house or its lord conjunct a malefic brings
  difficulty to the partner. `[safe]`
- **S 18.17, p.123** — a 7th lord devoid of strength and badly placed indicates
  loss or absence of partnership. `[gated_death]` where the text reads it as
  death; `[safe]` as "the marriage does not hold".
- **S 18.18, p.124** — the Moon in the 7th with the 7th lord afflicted: conjugal
  felicity is lacking. Santhanam notes the Moon here should be **waning**, which
  the verse does not state — a real reading dependency. `[safe]`

### The seventh lord through the houses (S 24.73–84)

| 7th lord in | Effect, gender-neutral | Class |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1st | (heading shloka; see 24.73) | `safe` |
| 2nd | gain of wealth through partnership; more than one union indicated | `safe` |
| 3rd | strain around children and siblings | `safe` |
| 4th | — | **excluded** (control framing) |
| 5th | honourable, well endowed | `safe` |
| 6th | the partner's health is a concern; friction with them | `safe` |
| 7th | endowed with happiness in marriage | `safe` |
| 8th | deprived of marital happiness | `safe` |
| 9th | many attachments indicated | `safe` |
| 10th | — | **excluded** (obedience framing) |
| 11th | gain of wealth through the partner | `safe` |
| 12th | expenditure; the marriage does not add to means | `safe` |

**Note the shape.** As with the 6th lord for health and the 10th for career,
**only about half of the twelve placements are actually about the marriage** —
the rest are wealth, siblings and children. The 7th lord is a significator of
partnership *and* of gain through others. Same lesson, third domain: read the
placement, do not assume the topic.

**Conventions (stated in every Upapada reference's `observance_note`).** The
Upapada is the arudha of the 12th house for every rising sign, as S 30.2 reads
and `jaimini.upapada()` computes. Santhanam's own note prefers the 2nd house
for an even rising sign, after some Jaimini commentaries; that variant is not
used. Aspects are Parashari graha drishti (`yogas._planet_aspects`), not Jaimini
sign aspects. Benefics are the natural benefics Jupiter, Venus and Mercury (UK's
note to IV.39); the Sun is a malefic only when not exalted, own or friendly
(S 30.6).

**Not ingested, deliberately.** Most of these verses are about losing a wife.
Left out: S 30.8-13 and UK IV.38-40's loss-of-wife, wife-count and lifespan
clauses; S 30.16-22, the wife's named illnesses; S 30.24-30, sons and daughters
read from the Upapada; S 30.9-10, many wives from Taurus or Gemini; the reverse
halves of S 30.14 (a debilitated lord, a low family) and UK IV.39 (a debilitated
malefic). Brihat Jataka's stri-jataka husband table (7th sign from the Navamsa
lagna) and Phaladeepika XI.3 were read and left out: both are rules for a
woman's chart only, and half of each is widowhood. BPHS 30.4's harsh half was
kept only because it has a neutral core (commitment is strained) and a stated
cancellation (30.5); it never reaches a reader in its own words.
